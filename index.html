Q1 · Natural, whole numbers and integers
1Sample Question

Q: Which of the following is an integer but not a whole number?

Natural numbers: 1, 2, 3, …  ·  Whole numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3, …  ·  Integers: …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, …
  • A) 0
  • B) 7
  • C) −4
  • D) 3/5
  • E) √2
  • F) 12

Answer: C (−4)

Explanation: Whole numbers start from 0 and never go negative. −4 is an integer, but it is negative, so it is not a whole number. 0, 7 and 12 are whole numbers, 3/5 is a fraction and √2 is irrational.

Now You Try

Q: Which of the following is a whole number but not a natural number?

Natural numbers: 1, 2, 3, …  ·  Whole numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3, …
Question 1 options
Remember: Natural numbers are 1, 2, 3, … and whole numbers are natural numbers together with 0. Every natural number is a whole number, every whole number is an integer, every integer is rational, and every rational number is real.
Q2 · Classifying numbers
2Sample Question

Q: From the numbers −4, 2/5, √7, 0, π, 6i, which are the irrational numbers?

Irrational numbers cannot be written as p/q. Imaginary numbers such as 6i are not real numbers at all.
  • A) √7 and π
  • B) 2/5 and √7
  • C) −4 and 0
  • D) π and 6i
  • E) √7 and 6i
  • F) 2/5 and π

Answer: A (√7 and π)

Explanation: Irrational numbers cannot be written as a fraction p/q. √7 and π are irrational. −4 = −4/1, 2/5 and 0 = 0/1 are rational, and 6i is imaginary.

Now You Try

Q: From the numbers −6, 5/8, √3, 0, 4.5, 9i, which are the rational numbers?

Rational numbers can be written as p/q with q ≠ 0, for example 0.5 = 1/2.
Question 2 options
Remember: Rational numbers can be written as p/q. Irrational numbers (such as √3 and π) cannot. Imaginary numbers (such as 5i) are not real. Rational and irrational numbers together make up the real numbers.
Q3 · Division by zero
3Sample Question

Q: Which of these expressions is undefined?

  • A) 0 ÷ 9
  • B) 9 ÷ 1
  • C) 9 ÷ 0
  • D) 0 × 9
  • E) 9 − 9
  • F) 0 + 9

Answer: C (9 ÷ 0)

Explanation: If 9 ÷ 0 = k, then k × 0 would have to equal 9. But any number multiplied by 0 gives 0, so no such number k exists. Division by zero is undefined.

Now You Try

Q: Which of the following statements is correct?

Question 3 options
Remember: Dividing by zero is undefined, because no number multiplied by 0 can give a non-zero answer. But dividing zero by a non-zero number is fine: 0 ÷ 5 = 0.
Q4 · Parallel and perpendicular line segments
4Sample Question

Q: ABCD is a rectangle. Its diagonals AC and BD are drawn as dashed lines. Which pair of line segments is parallel?

Parallel lines never meet. Perpendicular lines meet at 90°. Every angle of a rectangle is 90°.
  • A) AB and BC
  • B) AC and BD
  • C) AB and CD
  • D) AD and CD
  • E) AB and AC
  • F) BC and CD

Answer: C (AB and CD)

Explanation: Opposite sides of a rectangle are parallel, so AB ∥ CD (and AD ∥ BC). Sides that meet at a corner, such as AB and BC, are perpendicular. The diagonals AC and BD cross each other, so they are not parallel.

Now You Try

Q: PQRS is a rectangle. Its diagonals PR and QS are drawn as dashed lines. Which pair of line segments is perpendicular?

Parallel lines never meet. Perpendicular lines meet at 90°. Every angle of a rectangle is 90°.
Question 4 options
Remember: In a rectangle, opposite sides are parallel and sides meeting at a corner are perpendicular. A line segment has two endpoints, and each side of the rectangle is a line segment.
Q5 · Complementary and supplementary angles
5Sample Question

Q: An angle measures 38°. Find its complement and its supplement.

Complementary angles add up to 90°. Supplementary angles add up to 180°. A linear pair is supplementary.
  • A) 62° and 142°
  • B) 52° and 142°
  • C) 142° and 52°
  • D) 52° and 322°
  • E) 38° and 142°
  • F) 52° and 152°

Answer: B (52° and 142°)

Explanation: Complement = 90° − 38° = 52°. Supplement = 180° − 38° = 142°.

Now You Try

Q: An angle measures 57°. Find its complement and its supplement.

Complementary angles add up to 90°. Supplementary angles add up to 180°.
Question 5 options
Remember: Complementary angles add up to 90°, and supplementary angles add up to 180°. A linear pair (two adjacent angles on a straight line) is always supplementary.
Q6 · Vertically opposite angles
6Sample Question

Q: Lines AB and CD intersect at O and ∠AOC = 55°. Find ∠BOD, ∠AOD and ∠BOC.

  • A) ∠BOD = 55°, ∠AOD = 125°, ∠BOC = 125°
  • B) ∠BOD = 125°, ∠AOD = 55°, ∠BOC = 55°
  • C) ∠BOD = 55°, ∠AOD = 55°, ∠BOC = 125°
  • D) ∠BOD = 125°, ∠AOD = 125°, ∠BOC = 55°
  • E) ∠BOD = 35°, ∠AOD = 145°, ∠BOC = 145°
  • F) ∠BOD = 55°, ∠AOD = 125°, ∠BOC = 55°

Answer: A (∠BOD = 55°, ∠AOD = 125°, ∠BOC = 125°)

Explanation: ∠BOD is vertically opposite ∠AOC, so it is 55°. ∠AOD and ∠AOC form a linear pair, so ∠AOD = 180° − 55° = 125°. ∠BOC is vertically opposite ∠AOD, so it is also 125°.

Now You Try

Q: Lines PQ and RS intersect at O and ∠POS = 125°. Find ∠QOR, ∠POR and ∠QOS.

Question 6 options
Remember: Two intersecting lines form two pairs of equal vertically opposite angles, and every two adjacent angles add up to 180° (a linear pair).
Q7 · Intersecting lines and equations
★ Challenge
7Sample Question

Q: Lines AB and CD intersect at O. ∠AOC = 3x° and ∠AOD = 6x°. Find the value of x and ∠BOC.

Tip: ∠AOC and ∠AOD are adjacent angles on the straight line CD, so they form a linear pair.
  • A) x = 20, ∠BOC = 60°
  • B) x = 20, ∠BOC = 120°
  • C) x = 40, ∠BOC = 240°
  • D) x = 10, ∠BOC = 60°
  • E) x = 20, ∠BOC = 180°
  • F) x = 15, ∠BOC = 90°

Answer: B (x = 20, ∠BOC = 120°)

Explanation: ∠AOC and ∠AOD form a linear pair: 3x + 6x = 180°, so 9x = 180° and x = 20. Then ∠AOD = 6 × 20° = 120°. ∠BOC is vertically opposite ∠AOD, so ∠BOC = 120°.

Now You Try

Q: Lines PQ and RS intersect at O. ∠POR = 7x° and ∠POS = 8x°. Find the value of x and ∠QOS.

Tip: ∠POR and ∠POS are adjacent angles on the straight line RS, so they form a linear pair.
Question 7 options
Remember: Adjacent angles on a straight line add up to 180°, so write an equation in x and solve it. Then use vertically opposite angles (which are equal) to find the remaining angles.

  • F) ∠5 = 110° (corresponding), ∠3 = 70° (linear pair), ∠8 = 70° (alternate interior)

Explanation: ∠5 = ∠1 = 110° (corresponding angles). ∠3 = 180° − 110° = 70° (linear pair with ∠1). ∠8 = ∠1 = 110° (alternate exterior angles).

Now You Try

Remember: Use three facts: vertically opposite angles are equal, parallel lines make equal corresponding, alternate interior and alternate exterior angles, and a linear pair adds up to 180°.
Q9 · Angle at a bend
★ Challenge
9Sample Question

Q: In the figure, AB ∥ CD, ∠ABE = 140° and ∠CDE = 125°. Find ∠BED.

Tip: Draw a line through E parallel to AB and CD. It splits ∠BED into two parts.
  • A) 40°
  • B) 95°
  • C) 55°
  • D) 85°
  • E) 125°
  • F) 140°

Answer: B (∠BED = 95°)

Explanation: Draw a line through E parallel to AB and CD. Co-interior angles add up to 180°, giving 180° − 140° = 40° and 180° − 125° = 55°. So ∠BED = 40° + 55° = 95°.

Now You Try

Q: In the figure, PQ ∥ RS, ∠PQE = 150° and ∠RSE = 130°. Find ∠QES.

Tip: Draw a line through E parallel to PQ and RS. It splits ∠QES into two parts.
Question 9 options
Remember: To find an angle at a bend between two parallel lines, draw a line through the bend point parallel to both. Each part is found with co-interior angles (sum 180°) or alternate angles (equal), and the angle at the bend is the sum of the two parts.
Q10 · Several parallel lines
★ Challenge
10Sample Question

Q: In the figure, the lines l, m, n and p are parallel and t is a transversal cutting all of them. If ∠1 = 68°, find ∠2, ∠3 and ∠4.

Tip: Angles in the same position on every parallel line are equal (corresponding angles). Use linear pairs and vertically opposite angles to move between positions.
  • A) ∠2 = 112°, ∠3 = 112°, ∠4 = 68°
  • B) ∠2 = 112°, ∠3 = 68°, ∠4 = 112°
  • C) ∠2 = 68°, ∠3 = 112°, ∠4 = 112°
  • D) ∠2 = 68°, ∠3 = 68°, ∠4 = 112°
  • E) ∠2 = 112°, ∠3 = 68°, ∠4 = 68°
  • F) ∠2 = 68°, ∠3 = 112°, ∠4 = 68°

Answer: B (∠2 = 112°, ∠3 = 68°, ∠4 = 112°)

Explanation: ∠1 = 68°, so the angle below it on line l (a linear pair) is 112°. Corresponding angles are equal, so ∠2 = 112°. At line n, the upper right angle is 68° (corresponding to ∠1), and ∠3 is vertically opposite it, so ∠3 = 68°. At line p, the lower right angle is 112°, and ∠4 is vertically opposite it, so ∠4 = 112°.

Now You Try

Q: In the figure, the lines l, m, n and p are parallel and t is a transversal cutting all of them. If ∠1 = 124°, find ∠2, ∠3 and ∠4.

Tip: Angles in the same position on every parallel line are equal (corresponding angles). Use linear pairs and vertically opposite angles to move between positions.
Question 10 options
Remember: When many parallel lines are cut by one transversal, corresponding angles at every line are equal. A linear pair adds up to 180°, and vertically opposite angles are equal, so every angle is either the given angle or 180° minus it.
