Q1 · Number sets
1Sample Question

Q: Which statement about integers and rational numbers is correct?

  • A) Every integer is a rational number
  • B) No integer is a rational number
  • C) Every rational number is an integer
  • D) Every rational number is a natural number
  • E) Every integer is a natural number
  • F) Rational numbers and integers are the same set

Answer: A

Explanation: Every integer can be written as a fraction with denominator 1 (for example −3 = −3/1), so every integer is a rational number. Rational numbers such as ½ are not integers.

Question 1 options

Q2 · Parallel and perpendicular lines
2Sample Question

Q: In the figure, AB = AC and AD bisects ∠A. Which two line segments are perpendicular?

  • A) AB and BC
  • B) AB and AD
  • C) AC and AD
  • D) AD and BC
  • E) AB and AC
  • F) AC and BC

Answer: D (AD and BC)

Explanation: In an isosceles triangle, the bisector of the angle between the equal sides meets the base at 90°.

Q3 · Linear pair vs supplementary
3Sample Question

Q: Do a linear pair and a pair of complementary angles refer to the same thing?

Remember: Complementary angles add up to 90°. Supplementary angles add up to 180°.
  • A) Yes, they both add up to 180°
  • B) Yes, they both add up to 90°
  • C) No, a linear pair adds up to 180° but complementary angles add up to 90°
  • D) No, a linear pair adds up to 90° but complementary angles add up to 180°
  • E) Yes, they are both pairs of equal angles
  • F) Yes, they both add up to 360°

Answer: C

Explanation: A linear pair is two adjacent angles on a straight line, so it adds up to 180°. Complementary angles add up to 90°. The sums are different, so they are not the same thing.

Now You Try

Q: Do a linear pair and supplementary angles refer to the same thing?

Adjacent means side by side, sharing a vertex and one common arm.
Question 3 options
Remember: A linear pair is two adjacent angles on a straight line (sum 180°). Supplementary angles are any two angles that add up to 180°. Every linear pair is supplementary, but not every supplementary pair is a linear pair.
Q4 · Vertically opposite angles
4Sample Question

Q: Lines EF and GH intersect at O and ∠EOG = 70°. Find ∠EOH, ∠FOH and ∠FOG.

  • A) ∠EOH = 70°, ∠FOH = 110°, ∠FOG = 70°
  • B) ∠EOH = 110°, ∠FOH = 110°, ∠FOG = 70°
  • C) ∠EOH = 120°, ∠FOH = 60°, ∠FOG = 120°
  • D) ∠EOH = 70°, ∠FOH = 70°, ∠FOG = 110°
  • E) ∠EOH = 110°, ∠FOH = 70°, ∠FOG = 110°
  • F) ∠EOH = 110°, ∠FOH = 70°, ∠FOG = 70°

Answer: E (∠EOH = 110°, ∠FOH = 70°, ∠FOG = 110°)

Explanation: ∠FOH is vertically opposite to ∠EOG, so it is 70°. The linear pair gives 180° − 70° = 110° for ∠EOH, and ∠FOG is opposite to ∠EOH.

Now You Try

Q: Lines PQ and RS intersect at O and ∠POS = 115°. Find ∠POR, ∠QOR and ∠QOS.

Question 4 options
Remember: Two intersecting lines form two pairs of equal vertically opposite angles, and every two adjacent angles add up to 180°.
Q5 · Angle bisector
★ Challenge
5Sample Question

Q: Lines AB and CD intersect at O. ∠AOC = 64° and OE bisects ∠AOC. Find ∠EOB.

Bisects means cuts exactly in half. If OE bisects ∠AOC, then ∠AOE and ∠EOC are two equal angles.
  • A) 32°
  • B) 116°
  • C) 148°
  • D) 64°
  • E) 96°
  • F) 138°

Answer: C (∠EOB = 148°)

Explanation: OE bisects ∠AOC, so ∠AOE = 64° ÷ 2 = 32°. A, O and B lie on a straight line, so ∠AOB = 180°. Therefore ∠EOB = 180° − ∠AOE = 180° − 32° = 148°.

Now You Try

Q: Lines PQ and RS intersect at O. ∠POR = 50° and OT bisects ∠QOR. Find ∠TOS.

Bisects means cuts exactly in half. If OT bisects ∠QOR, then ∠QOT and ∠TOR are two equal angles.
Question 5 options
Remember: To bisect an angle means to cut it into two equal parts, so each half is half of the whole angle. Use linear pairs (180°) and vertically opposite angles (equal) to find the other angles, then add the parts you need.
Q6 · Angle pairs with a transversal
6Sample Question

Q: In the figure, l ∥ m and t is a transversal. What type of angle pair are ∠2 and ∠6?

  • A) Corresponding angles
  • B) Alternate interior angles
  • C) Co-interior angles
  • D) Alternate exterior angles
  • E) Vertically opposite angles
  • F) Linear pair

Answer: A (Corresponding angles)

Explanation: ∠2 and ∠6 are in the same position at the two lines (above each line, on the right of the transversal), so they form an F-shape. They are corresponding angles.

Now You Try

Q: In the figure, l ∥ m and t is a transversal. What type of angle pair are ∠1 and ∠8?

Question 6 options
Remember: Corresponding angles form an F-shape, alternate angles a Z-shape and co-interior angles a C-shape. F and Z give equal angles; C gives a sum of 180°.
Q7 · Equal angles with parallel lines
7Sample Question

Q: In the figure, l ∥ m and ∠4 = 120°. Which of these angles is also equal to 120°?

  • A) ∠2
  • B) ∠3
  • C) ∠6
  • D) ∠7
  • E) ∠5
  • F) None of these

Answer: E (∠5)

Explanation: ∠5 and ∠4 are alternate interior angles (Z-shape), so ∠5 = ∠4 = 120°. The other angles ∠2, ∠3, ∠6 and ∠7 are each 60°.

Now You Try

Q: In the figure, l ∥ m and ∠7 = 70°. Which of these angles is also equal to 70°?

Question 7 options
Remember: When two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, corresponding, alternate interior and alternate exterior angles are equal, and co-interior angles add up to 180°.
Q8 · Finding angles with parallel lines
8Sample Question

Q: In the figure, l ∥ m and ∠1 = 128°. Find ∠2, ∠5 and ∠7.

  • A) ∠2 = 52°, ∠5 = 128°, ∠7 = 52°
  • B) ∠2 = 128°, ∠5 = 52°, ∠7 = 128°
  • C) ∠2 = 52°, ∠5 = 52°, ∠7 = 128°
  • D) ∠2 = 62°, ∠5 = 118°, ∠7 = 62°
  • E) ∠2 = 52°, ∠5 = 128°, ∠7 = 128°
  • F) ∠2 = 128°, ∠5 = 128°, ∠7 = 52°

Answer: A (∠2 = 52°, ∠5 = 128°, ∠7 = 52°)

Explanation: ∠2 = 180° − 128° = 52° (linear pair). ∠5 = ∠1 = 128° (corresponding angles). ∠7 = ∠2 = 52° (alternate exterior angles).

Now You Try

Q: In the figure, l ∥ m and ∠4 = 115°. Find ∠3, ∠5 and ∠8.

Question 8 options
Remember: Use three facts: vertically opposite angles are equal, parallel lines make equal alternate and corresponding angles, and a linear pair adds up to 180°.
Q9 · Angle at a bend
★ Challenge
9Sample Question

Q: In the figure, AB ∥ CD, ∠ABE = 145° and ∠CDE = 115°. Find ∠BED.

  • A) 35°
  • B) 65°
  • C) 100°
  • D) 80°
  • E) 115°
  • F) 145°

Answer: C (∠BED = 100°)

Explanation: Draw a line through E parallel to AB and CD. Co-interior angles add up to 180°, giving 180° − 145° = 35° and 180° − 115° = 65°. So ∠BED = 35° + 65° = 100°.

Now You Try

Q: In the figure, PQ ∥ RS, ∠PQE = 135° and ∠RSE = 105°. Find ∠QES.

Question 9 options
Remember: To find an angle at a bend between two parallel lines, draw a line through the bend point parallel to both. Each part is found with alternate angles (equal) or co-interior angles (sum 180°), and the angle at the bend is the sum of the two parts.
Q10 · Double bend
★ Challenge
10Sample Question

Q: In the figure, PQ ∥ RS. The path QEFS bends at E and at F. ∠PQE = 145°, ∠EFS = 115° and ∠RSF = 45°. Find ∠QEF.

Tip: Draw a line through each bend point (E and F) parallel to PQ and RS. Each bend angle is split into two parts.
  • A) 75°
  • B) 95°
  • C) 105°
  • D) 115°
  • E) 35°
  • F) 70°

Answer: C (∠QEF = 105°)

Explanation: At Q, 180° − 145° = 35° (co-interior angles), which is the upper part of ∠QEF. At F, the lower part of ∠EFS equals ∠RSF = 45° (alternate angles), so the upper part is 115° − 45° = 70°. By alternate angles, this 70° is also the lower part of ∠QEF. So ∠QEF = 35° + 70° = 105°.

Now You Try

Q: In the figure, PQ ∥ RS. The path QEFS bends at E and at F. ∠PQE = 135°, ∠QEF = 100° and ∠RSF = 40°. Find ∠EFS.

Tip: Draw a line through each bend point (E and F) parallel to PQ and RS. Each bend angle is split into two parts.
Question 10 options
Remember: For a zigzag between two parallel lines, draw a line through every bend point parallel to both. Each bend angle splits into an upper part and a lower part, and alternate angles carry each part along to the next bend. Add the two parts to get the angle you need.
